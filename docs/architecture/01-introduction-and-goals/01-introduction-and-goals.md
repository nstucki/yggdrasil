# 1. Introduction and Goals

_Part of [Yggdrasil — Architecture (arc42)](../README.md) · [§1](README.md)._

## 1.1 Requirements Overview

This document records the architecture of the Yggdrasil orchestration framework — the whole repository rooted at the project directory. Yggdrasil is not an application: it is a configuration layer for OpenCode that installs agent definitions, skills, and commands into the OpenCode configuration directory and encodes an orchestration doctrine — one orchestrator (Odin) delegating to five role-bound specialist subagents, exchanging transient Workfiles, gated by independent review, and backed by a persistent, reviewed knowledge base (`README.md:1-471`; `agents/odin-autonomous.md:22-44`).

**Documented scope.** The entire repository: `agents/` (8 generated agent definitions), `skills/` (feature directories of `SKILL.md` files), `commands/yggdrasil/` (8 command definitions), `scripts/` (generators, validator, smoke tests, template sources), `config-home/` (install-side payload), `setup.sh` (installer), `docs/architecture/` (this document), and `README.md`. The OpenCode host framework, the internal workflow steps of individual skills beyond their boundary contracts, the install-side generated `capability-inventory` skill, and CI mechanics are out of scope.

**Evidence base.** Every claim in this document cites a repository path. The as-is baseline was drafted from a structural survey of the whole repository plus direct reads of `agents/odin-autonomous.md`, `agents/brokk.md`, `scripts/README.md`, `scripts/generate-subagents.sh:1-145`, `README.md:90-179`, and directory listings of `agents/`, `skills/**`, `commands/yggdrasil/`, `scripts/**`, `config-home/`. The architecture-documentation requirements below were drafted from a business analysis of the documentation objective and direct reads of the five `skills/architecture/*/SKILL.md` files, `skills/engineering/odin-engineering-workflow/SKILL.md`, `agents/odin-autonomous.md` § Workflows, `scripts/odin-generator/shared-body.template.md` § Workflows, `setup.sh:345-394`, and `README.md:265-301`. The TDD-workflow requirements below are recorded from the workflow's own skill text — the six `skills/tdd/*/SKILL.md` files and the two `skills/engineering/*/SKILL.md` files — together with `agents/brokk.md:6-52`, `agents/kvasir.md:26-33`, `agents/odin-autonomous.md:211-229, 306-313` (the TDD and Software Engineering trigger sections and the Trigger Thresholds), `commands/yggdrasil/tdd.md:1-13`, `scripts/odin-generator/shared-body.template.md:150, 184-202`, and `setup.sh:167-174, 361-372`. The quality goals of the system in §1.2 are inferred and marked as such; the quality goals of the architecture-documentation subsystem are stated requirements.

The requirements below are the durable statement of those obligations; they are the criteria the decisions ADR-0009 – ADR-0018 and ADR-0024 realize and the §10.2 scenarios QS-9 – QS-17, QS-24, and QS-25 measure. DOC-1, DOC-5, DOC-6, and DOC-9 are restated to the workflow's current form; DOC-10 – DOC-13 were added for it; DOC-14 and DOC-15 record the two narrow writes the persistence step may make to files it already wrote.

**Requirements on the TDD-workflow subsystem.** The TDD workflow must turn a bounded change, or the work packages of a ratified architecture, into code that reaches a target project's history only as reviewed, green commits, through one workflow form — the same whether invoked standalone or as a stage — whose only unreviewed node is the plan step. The requirements below are the durable statement of those obligations, recorded from the skill text of the six skills under `skills/tdd/` that form the workflow, from the Odin trigger sections that fire it, and from the Software Engineering workflow that runs it as a stage; they are the criteria ADR-0019 – ADR-0023 realize and the §10.2 scenarios QS-18 – QS-23 measure. TDD-1 is restated to the workflow's current entry points; TDD-11 and TDD-12 are added for them.

| ID | Requirement | Realized by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TDD-1 | The TDD workflow is a standalone packaged workflow invoked whole — by the `/yggdrasil/tdd` command, by its `TDD check` trigger verdict, or as a stage of the Software Engineering workflow — with identical mechanics on every path, no request line and no result line, and only the destination of its Response differing; its Deliverable is workflow-fixed as `Deliverable: response=yes, artifact=yes — code and tests committed in the target project on the plan's branch (…), source=workflow-fixed` | ADR-0019, ADR-0023 |
| TDD-2 | The Software Engineering workflow's shape verdict decides analysis and architecture only; behavioral context is the TDD stage's own conditional gate; the enclosing run passes into the stage only the objective and the Workfiles it produced — never packages, modes, batches, or commit instructions | ADR-0019 |
| TDD-3 | The plan step receives no review. Its gates are the plan checkpoint, which ratifies it, and the reviews of every session that executes it; every plan ships at `Status: Proposed`; the planner resolves exactly two repository facts — the current branch and the working-tree state — and runs no tests | ADR-0021 |
| TDD-4 | Packages are taken verbatim from a ratified architecture Workfile's Appendix B, or exactly one package is derived when none exists — a second package without an architecture Workfile stops the run; the five parallel criteria are applied once, as the planner's judgment against the live tree, and the enclosing orchestration only records the resulting `Package check:` line | ADR-0021 |
| TDD-5 | No session other than a `brokk-tdd-commit` session commits: every implementing and integrating session stops at a refactored green state, uncommitted, with `Commits: none` in its evidence block, and a `HEAD` moved since the pinned reference is a blocking finding at its review | ADR-0020 |
| TDD-6 | A commit is recorded only when every review the batch's `Gate` names reads `PASS` or `PASS-WITH-NOTES`, by a fresh session that re-reads those verdict lines itself, verifies `HEAD` against the pinned reference, stages exactly the union of the reviewed `Files changed` by explicit path, re-runs the full suite on the combined tree, commits with the plan row's type and subject, and is itself reviewed under `Focus: commit` | ADR-0020 |
| TDD-7 | Every batch boundary is a verifiable green tree state: a parallel wave plus its integration is one batch; consecutive sequential packages share a batch only when their write sets are pairwise disjoint; a scaffold is its own batch, committed before fan-out; no `Phase: red` result is a batch boundary; and a batch commits before the first package of the next batch is dispatched | ADR-0022 |
| TDD-8 | Commit types are the four `feat:`, `fix:`, `refactor:`, `test:`, mapped from package kind in exactly one normative place; commits land only on a branch prefixed `feature/`, `fix/`, or `refactor/`, created by the commit session when the plan says so, and never on `main`, `master`, `develop`, or an unprefixed branch | ADR-0022 |
| TDD-9 | The workflow never rewrites history and never reverts, stashes, or deletes working-tree changes: a blocked commit is corrected by a disclosed corrective commit, and work left uncommitted by a blocked run stays in the tree and is disclosed | ADR-0020 |
| TDD-10 | The TDD review skill has exactly four Focus values — `context`, `package`, `integration`, `commit` — and reviews no architecture artifact; a TDD-context Workfile is never a skip reason for the Architecture workflow's context gate, and an architecture-context Workfile is never one for the TDD workflow's | ADR-0019, ADR-0020 |
| TDD-11 | Exactly one of the TDD check and the Engineering check owns any request: explicit test-driven-development, test-first, or red-green-refactor language *without* requirements-analysis or architecture-before-implementation intent → TDD check invoke, Engineering check skip; the same language *with* that intent, or a change that introduces structure or splits into two or more work packages → Engineering check invoke, TDD check skip; a bounded, testably-stated change that fits the existing structure in one package, without test-first language, is the TDD check's suggestion candidate and never the Engineering check's; a change that introduces structure, without workflow language, is the Engineering check's suggestion candidate and never the TDD check's | ADR-0023 |

## 1.2 Quality Goals

**Quality goals of the system.** All five are **inferred** from what the structure, permission blocks, validators, and doctrine text are visibly optimized for. Derivation basis: each goal is named only where at least two independent mechanisms in the repository enforce it. Ranking reflects how much of the doctrine text and permission machinery is devoted to each.

| Rank | Quality goal | What it means here | Inferred from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Independent verification** | No output reaches the user, the project, Memory, or the project's history without a review by a reviewer that did not produce it, verified against ground truth rather than self-report | `agents/odin-autonomous.md:247-275` (§ Review & Quality Gates: Review Mechanics, Subtask Review, Final Review Gate); `agents/odin-autonomous.md:40` and `agents/brokk.md:88` (no self-review); `skills/memories/odin-memory-system/SKILL.md:57`; `skills/architecture/odin-architecture-workflow/SKILL.md:14, 63-70`; `skills/tdd/odin-tdd-workflow/SKILL.md:14, 61, 87-88` (§ Purpose; § Workflow step 6; § Quality Criteria) |
| 2 | **Role isolation and least privilege** | Each agent may do only what its role needs; boundaries are enforced by tool-permission blocks, skill-prefix allowlists, and a validator that forbids cross-agent references | `agents/odin-autonomous.md:6-19` (Odin: only `skill`, `task`, `todo`); `agents/mimir.md:6-68`; `agents/brokk.md:6-64`; `scripts/README.md:71` (Check 5, subagent isolation) |
| 3 | **Reproducibility of the definitions** | Agent files are derived artifacts; the committed state must be byte-identical to generator output | `scripts/README.md:70, 87-95, 105-107, 153-160`; `scripts/generate-subagents.sh:103-121` |
| 4 | **Traceability of claims** | Research, reviews, and Memories cite paths and lines; a count is arithmetic over an enumeration that is its proof — enumerated first, its unit and method labelled, its categories partitioned or reconciled; Memories carry a mandatory `sources` field and a confidence/status lifecycle; a commit's path set equals the union of the reviewed diffs it records | `skills/research/mimir-research-convention/SKILL.md` § Proof Requirements (Proof Principles; Counts and Enumerations); `skills/memories/brokk-memory-curation/SKILL.md:91-110`; `agents/odin-autonomous.md:256`; `agents/brokk.md:111-113`; `skills/tdd/brokk-tdd-commit/SKILL.md:10, 76` |
| 5 | **Extensibility at the install site** | Skills and custom tool grants are added in the installed configuration home without modifying the repository; the capability inventory is regenerated there | `README.md:106, 118, 122, 156`; `config-home/generate-capabilities.sh`; `config-home/custom-capabilities.yaml`; `scripts/README.md:162-166` |

**Quality goals of the architecture-documentation subsystem.** These five are **stated requirements** (not inferred) on the Architecture workflow's output. They drive ADR-0009 – ADR-0018 and ADR-0024: every options table in the decided records scores its options against these goals by name, §4 names a tactic per goal, and §10.2 carries scenarios per goal. They refine, and do not re-rank, the system goals above; the subsystem block exists because none of the five system goals speaks to what a persisted document should contain.

| Rank | Quality goal | What it means here | Measurable target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Durability** | A persisted architecture document remains a valid reference across the tasks that follow the one that triggered it, without edits to remove that task's details | A document authored for task T1 remains valid for T2, T3, … with zero removals of T1-specific content |
| 2 | **Clarity** | A reader distinguishes stable architectural facts from task-scoped detail, and knows why a section is empty, without re-reading the triggering objective | Every omitted section carries a typed marker; no persisted section mixes run provenance with architecture |
| 3 | **Maintainability** | Updating the document for a new objective touches only what the objective changes and carries the rest forward; correcting one persisted document touches that document, its indices, and nothing else; superseding a record touches its status line and nothing else | A delta that changes one building block re-authors one topic document, not its whole section; a correction changes exactly the named documents plus their indices; a supersession changes exactly one record line and one §9 cell |
| 4 | **Consistency** | One relevance rule and one workflow form apply in both document scopes and all twelve sections — there is no mode, so there is no mode-specific exemption | One test, stated once, referenced by the author and the gatherer, its pattern-matchable subset checked mechanically at persistence; zero scope-specific exemptions |
| 5 | **Usability** | Implementation sessions read §5 and §9 as the contract they must respect and find no implementation-phase guidance that belongs in their own work package | §5 blackbox entries carry interfaces and invariants; zero helper-structure, naming, or test-selection statements |

**No goal block is added for the TDD-workflow subsystem.** Its decisions ADR-0019 – ADR-0023 are driven by system goals 1 (independent verification — of history, not only of Workfiles and Artifacts), 2 (role isolation — judgment and mechanics in separate sessions), and 4 (traceability — commit ⇔ reviewed union), and by the subsystem goals Consistency (every packaged workflow composes the same way and every request has exactly one trigger owner) and Maintainability (one grammar owner per hand-off; one workflow body serving both entry paths), all ranked above. The rule of §11 R15 therefore holds: a subsystem block is added only when a delta's decisions are driven by goals absent from the ranking.
